Circuit Trace 1.9.9: 3D Cube, flow puzzles on all six sides

📅 14 November 2026⏱ 4 min read✍️ Grounded Studio

Version 1.9.9 brings the most unusual pack Circuit Trace has had so far: 3D Cube, 100 flow puzzles that wrap around all six sides of a cube. It also gives the game a new icon and fixes a scrolling bug on the Puzzles screen. Here is what's new, and how a 2D puzzle game ended up with a cube in it.

New: 3D Cube

The rules are the ones every flow puzzle player knows: connect each pair of matching dots, never cross another line, and fill every cell. The difference is the board. Each side of the cube is a small grid, and the six sides are joined at their edges. Draw a line to the edge of one face and it carries straight on to the next.

You see three sides at a time. Swipe an empty spot to turn the cube, or drag a line off the edge towards a side you can't see: the cube rolls round and your line goes with it, without lifting your finger. The first level shows both gestures with an animated hand.

The pack has 10 sectors. The first five use a 3×3 grid on every side (54 cells), the last five a 4×4 grid (96 cells). Within each size the number of colours goes down, so the lines get longer and wrap around more sides. 3D Cube is free, under GAME MODES → 3D CUBE.

How we built a cube in a 2D game

Circuit Trace is a 2D game, and we didn't want to rebuild it in 3D just for one mode. It turned out we didn't have to. The cube is drawn without perspective, so each visible face is simply a flat grid that has been slanted and squashed. Every face gets one 2D transform, and the normal board drawing code paints straight onto it. Paths, glow, themes and the FX packs all work on the cube because they're the same code.

The puzzle logic needed even less. To the game, a cube is one long strip of six faces, plus a table that says which cells touch across the edges. Everything else (dragging, undo, hints, the scoring) works exactly as on a flat board.

"A cube is just six boards that agree on who their neighbours are."

The levels come from the same generator and puzzle solver as the rest of the game, now fed the cube's neighbour table. Each level was checked to have a single clean solution and rated on how much thinking it takes, then all 100 were played through automatically before release. The first prototype made you let go of a line whenever the cube turned. On a real phone that felt choppy, so the final version keeps the line in your finger the whole way round.

A new icon, and a fix

  • New app icon. Three neon lines on a grid inside a hexagon, each connecting its own pair without crossing. It finally shows what the game is about at a glance.
  • Puzzles screen scrolls again. With seven packs the list is longer than most screens, and a drag that started on a pack card didn't scroll. It does now.
  • The one-time "what's new" message now introduces 3D Cube, with a button that takes you straight to it.

🧊 New to the cube?

Turn it all the way round before you draw anything. A cube has no corners or edges to lean on, so a dot on the back is often what decides your first move. More tips are in the 3D Cube guide on circuittrace.app.
