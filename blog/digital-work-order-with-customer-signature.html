Digital Work Order with Customer Signature: Get Every Job Signed Off on Site

📅 5 November 2026⏱ 6 min read✍️ Grounded Studio

The job is done, the client is happy, you drive off. Three weeks later the invoice arrives and suddenly nobody remembers the extra socket, the two hours of waiting for the delivery or the tiles you had to buy twice. A work order signed on the spot prevents exactly that conversation.

What is a work order?

A work order (also called a job sheet or service report) is a short record of what you did on a job: the work carried out, the hours, the materials used and, ideally, the client's signature. It is not a quote and not an invoice. It sits in between: the quote says what you will do, the work order says what you did, and the invoice asks to be paid for it.

Why a signature makes the difference

  • Extra work is agreed in writing. "While you're here, can you also..." is the most common source of payment disputes. If it is on the signed work order, it was agreed.
  • Hours are no longer a discussion. The client saw and signed the hours on the day, not three weeks later on an invoice.
  • Faster payment. A client who signed off the work has already said yes to the content of your invoice.
  • Proof for yourself. Photos plus a signature show the state you left the job in, useful if a problem shows up months later.

What belongs on a work order

FieldWhy
Project, client and dateSo it is clear which job and which day it covers
Work carried outShort lines, one per task; include extra work separately
HoursTotal per day, ideally from a timer rather than from memory
Materials usedQuantities, so the invoice matches what went into the job
PhotosBefore and after, or anything that was hidden behind finishing
Name and signature of the clientThe actual sign-off

💡 Paper works too, but...

A paper job sheet gets wet, gets lost in the van and has to be typed into your invoice later. A digital work order is a PDF you can share straight away and keep with the project.
