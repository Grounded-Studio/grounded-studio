Your Food Waste Wrapped: A Spotify-Style Year in Review for Your Fridge

📅 July 27, 2026 ⏱ 5 min read ✍️ Grounded Studio

Every December, Spotify tells you which song you played 847 times. Apple Health tells you how many steps you walked. Duolingo tells you you're in the top 3% of Portuguese learners in Belgium. But nobody tells you the one thing that actually affects your wallet and the planet: how much food you saved from the bin this year.

So I built it. FreshGarant's new Year in Review is a 7-slide recap that arrives at the end of every calendar year — a shareable, no-hype breakdown of your fridge stats. Here's what's in it and why I think it's the most honest "wrapped" you'll see all year.

01

Products tracked this year

The headline number: everything you scanned, added or captured with the AI photo tool over the past 12 months. For most active users this lands somewhere between 200 and 1,200 items — a surprisingly good proxy for how deliberately you shop.

Example: 487 products tracked
02

Money saved from the bin

Every product you finished before it expired = money that didn't go to waste. FreshGarant estimates the value based on the categories you tracked and the Dutch average price for each. It's an estimate, not an exact ledger — but it's usually the slide people screenshot.

Example: €217 saved
03

Kilos of food rescued

Money is one thing; the physical weight is another. Seeing "27 kg of food rescued" in one number puts the impact in perspective. That's roughly the weight of a large suitcase — of food that would otherwise have been binned.

Example: 27 kg rescued
04

CO₂ emissions avoided

Producing food that never gets eaten emits carbon anyway. Using the Voedingscentrum's published figure (~2.5 kg CO₂ per kg of avoided waste), FreshGarant calculates your personal CO₂ footprint reduction for the year. This is the slide climate-anxious users tend to share.

Example: 68 kg CO₂ avoided
05

Your top-tracked product

The one item you scan every single week. For most Dutch users it's milk, eggs or yoghurt. For me it turned out to be Greek yoghurt — I add a new pot every Sunday like clockwork. It's oddly satisfying to see your own patterns laid out.

Example: Milk (52 scans)
06

Longest streak

The longest run of consecutive days you opened the app or added a product. It's not about guilt — it's about showing that consistency, more than any single feature, is what actually reduces waste over time.

Example: 43-day streak
07

A shareable summary card

The final slide combines everything into a single image sized for Instagram Stories and WhatsApp. One tap, saved to your camera roll. Share it or don't — the point is that your yearly progress is visible in a format people actually engage with.

Example: 1080×1920 story-ready image

Why a "wrapped" for food actually makes sense

The success of Spotify Wrapped isn't the design — it's the psychology. Feedback loops. Progress made visible. A moment of "huh, look at that." Food waste is invisible by nature: it happens quietly at the back of the fridge and disappears into the compost bin. Nobody tracks it. Nobody sees it.

Year in Review makes it visible. Once a year, in a format that takes 90 seconds to swipe through. That's it. No streaks to maintain, no leaderboard, no dopamine loop. Just a small, honest look at what you actually did — and, hopefully, motivation to do a bit more next year.

What if I haven't been tracking?

If you install FreshGarant in November, your December Year in Review will be short. That's fine — think of it as a baseline. The one that hits differently is the second year, when you can compare. Most users see their "kilos rescued" number roughly double from year one to year two, purely because they got used to the habit.

How the numbers are calculated (transparent version)

I get uncomfortable when apps show impressive stats without saying where they came from. So, briefly:

  • Products tracked: literal count from your local database.
  • Money saved: sum of estimated value for each product marked as "finished" (i.e. used before expiring). Prices come from a category-level average based on Dutch supermarket data.
  • Kilos rescued: average weight per product category × number of finished items.
  • CO₂ avoided: kilos rescued × 2.5 (Voedingscentrum's published multiplier for avoided food waste emissions).
  • Streak: consecutive calendar days with at least one app action.

None of these numbers are precise to the cent or gram — they're estimates. But the methodology is public, the multipliers are cited, and the goal is directional feedback, not accounting.

A small note

I built this feature after a friend asked me, half-joking, whether FreshGarant would ever have a "wrapped moment." She was right — the data was already there, it just needed a moment. If yours ends up being useful, share it. If not, no worries. Either way, thanks for tracking.
