I Compared Supermarket Prices Automatically for 60 Days — Here's What I Found

📅 July 27, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ Grounded Studio

Two months ago, I switched on FreshGarant's new price-tracking feature. The premise is simple: every time you scan a product's barcode after shopping, the app remembers the price and the store. Over time you get a price history per product, per supermarket — and the app quietly flags when a product is unusually cheap or expensive.

I did it for 60 days across Albert Heijn, Jumbo, Lidl and Aldi. The results were surprising, occasionally frustrating, and useful enough that I now shop differently than I did in May.

The setup (roughly 3 seconds per product)

You don't have to do anything special. You scan a barcode like you normally would in FreshGarant, tap "add price" once, type the number from your receipt, and pick the store. That's it. After a few weeks of this, the app has enough data to start showing you patterns.

My data set after 60 days: 216 unique products, tracked across 4 supermarkets, over 11 shopping trips. Not exhaustive. But enough to see the shape.

Finding 1: The "cheap" supermarket wins less often than you think

Everyone assumes Lidl and Aldi win on everything. They don't. Of the 60 products where I had prices from all four stores, here's how the "cheapest" prize was distributed:

StoreCheapest on X products% of total
Lidl2440%
Aldi1728%
Jumbo1118%
Albert Heijn814%

Yes, discount supermarkets win most often — but Albert Heijn and Jumbo were cheapest on 32% of items when they had promos running. The takeaway isn't "always shop at Lidl." It's "the cheapest place depends on the product." Which is exactly why manually comparing across stores never works — the mental overhead is too high.

Finding 2: Branded staples are where the price gaps live

The biggest swings weren't on random items — they were on branded staples people buy every week. Some real examples from my tracking:

ProductCheapestMost expensiveGap
Coca-Cola 1.5LLidl €1.79AH €2.35+31%
Nutella 400gJumbo €3.29AH €4.19+27%
Heinz Tomato KetchupAldi €2.09AH €2.99+43%

If you buy these five items every week, that's roughly €8–€12 per week of avoidable overspend. Or, put differently, €40–€60/month you could keep by shifting three items to a different supermarket.

Finding 3: Store-brand staples are basically identical in price

Interestingly, the home-brand basics — pasta, tinned tomatoes, rice, sugar, plain yoghurt — barely varied across stores. Usually within 5 cents of each other. So if your shop is mostly home-brands, chasing the "cheapest supermarket" isn't going to save you meaningful money. The wins are in branded items and fresh produce.

Finding 4: "Deal alerts" caught things I would have missed

The app has an automatic deal detector: when a product's current price is at least 15% below its rolling average, it flags it. This turned out to be more useful than I expected. Twice in the 60 days it pinged me about coffee being 30% cheaper than usual — I bought two bags each time and saved roughly €4 per bag. Small, but it stacks.

Free tip: track just your top 10 products

You don't need to track every item to benefit. Pick the 10 things you buy most often — usually staples like milk, bread, cheese, coffee, laundry detergent, and a handful of favourite branded items. Track those consistently for a month. That alone will show you where to shop for what, and it's where 80% of the savings live.

What I changed after 60 days

I don't shop-hop every day. Nobody has time for that. But three small habits stuck:

  • Branded staples → Lidl or Jumbo once a month. I do one bigger monthly trip specifically for the branded items where the gap is biggest. €30–€40 saved reliably per trip.
  • Fresh produce → whichever is closest. Fruit and veg prices are close enough between stores that convenience beats optimisation.
  • Deal alerts → automatic. If FreshGarant pings me about coffee being unusually cheap, I stock up. If it doesn't, I don't think about it.

The honest limitations

Price tracking isn't magic. You have to enter prices manually (the app can't read your receipt yet — that's a separate PRO feature). You'll miss data for products you only buy at one store. And the "cheapest" store changes when promotions rotate — so the data goes stale if you stop scanning. All of which is fine, as long as you know that going in.

The real value isn't in any single price comparison. It's in the pattern — knowing which handful of items are worth going out of your way for, and which aren't. That takes weeks of data. And that's exactly what the app is quietly doing while you shop like you already were.
