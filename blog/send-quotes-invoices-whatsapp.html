Sending Quotes and Invoices via WhatsApp — What's Allowed and How to Keep It Professional

📅 27 September 2026⏱ 7 min read✍️ Grounded Studio

Client texts you: "Can you WhatsApp me that quote?" You hesitate. Is that even allowed? Does it work as well as email? And if the client just replies with a thumbs-up — is that a yes?

Short answer: yes, sending a quote via WhatsApp is fine legally, and for sole traders it usually works better than email. But there are a few things you need to do to keep it professional and enforceable. In this guide: the legal basis (in the Dutch context), how to compose a clean message, why "read" isn't the same as "agreed", and how to fix the mobile number format in one tap.

Is a quote via WhatsApp legally valid?

Under Dutch law a quote (offerte) is nothing more than an offer to enter into a contract. The law doesn't prescribe a form — verbal is fine, email is fine, WhatsApp is fine too. What matters is that the offer is clear and that the client accepts it.

If a dispute arises later, the WhatsApp conversation counts as evidence. Dutch courts accept screenshots as long as it's clear who sent which message and when. So keep the original chat — don't wipe it once a job is done. If a client challenges you, you'll want to be able to pull it up.

The biggest win: your quote actually gets opened. Residential clients regularly let email disappear into folders they never revisit. WhatsApp pings and gets read. For smaller jobs (up to a few thousand euros) that's often the difference between "yes, go ahead" and radio silence.

Why "read" (blue ticks) is not agreement

This is where it goes wrong. You send the PDF. Blue ticks appear. You get to work. Two weeks later the client refuses to pay and says: "But I never actually said yes." Legally, they have a point — passive reading isn't acceptance.

What does count as agreement:

  • A clear text confirmation: "Agreed", "Go ahead", "Sounds good, go for it"
  • A thumbs-up emoji in reply to the direct question "shall we go ahead?" — that combination is valid
  • A partial payment matching what's in the quote
  • A written scheduling confirmation (your message "I'll plan it in for Thursday" + their reply)

What doesn't count: blue ticks alone, "OK I'll read it later", or a reply to only one line of the quote.

💡 Always ask explicitly for confirmation

After the PDF, send one line: "Let me know if you're happy with it and I'll put it in the schedule?" If you get "yes" back you have a written acceptance. No reply after three days? Send a friendly nudge.

What a clean message looks like

A solid quote message contains: greeting, reference to the conversation, short summary, PDF attachment, validity period, and a concrete call-to-action. Example:

"Hi Peter, as discussed, here's the quote for the roof windows. Total comes to €2,850 including VAT, materials and labour, valid until 15 October. PDF attached. Let me know if you'd like me to go ahead and I'll schedule it in for week 42. Cheers, Ronald."

Why this works: name used, reference to the conversation so the context is locked in, total amount stated in the text itself (client doesn't even need to open the PDF for the ballpark), validity date, and a request for confirmation with a concrete next step.

From 06 number to 316 format

WhatsApp runs on international numbers. Your client is saved in your phone as "06-12345678" but WhatsApp expects "+31 6 12345678" (or "316" without the plus). You used to have to reformat that manually. Now the app does it for you.

⚠️ Business clients: email plus WhatsApp

For larger jobs (main contractors, VvE homeowners' associations, commercial premises), email is still the convention. Send the PDF by email and follow up with a short WhatsApp: "PDF is in your inbox — let me know." That combines formal proof with fast response.

What to do if the client just sends a thumbs-up

Don't ignore it and don't assume it's a yes. Reply with a concrete next step: "Great, I'll schedule it for Thursday 3 October — does that work?" If you get "yes" back, now you do have explicit confirmation. A standalone thumbs-up remains legally ambiguous — since a 2023 Canadian ruling it's sometimes treated as acceptance, but don't rely on it.

Summary

A quote via WhatsApp is legally fine, gets opened more reliably than email, and fits the way sole traders actually work. Always ask for an explicit text confirmation — "read" isn't the same as "agreed". Keep the message personal but professional: greeting, summary, amount, validity, next step. Save the chat as evidence. For larger commercial jobs, combine with an email.
