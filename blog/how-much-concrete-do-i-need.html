How Much Concrete Do I Need? Calculating m³ for Slabs, Foundations and Driveways

📅 27 September 2026⏱ 7 min read✍️ Grounded Studio

Concrete is one of the most annoying materials to order. Too little and the mixer truck has to come back for half a cubic metre — call-out fee, delayed pour, cold joint in the slab. Too much and you're stuck with a half-cured mass you need to dump somewhere within an hour and a half.

In this guide: how to calculate m³ of concrete for a slab, strip foundation and driveway, why you always order 5–10% extra, and when ready-mix from a truck beats mixing bags yourself. Written for the Dutch context (Nederlandse leverancier, standard delivery increments of 0.5 m³), but the maths is universal.

The basic formula

Volume calculation
Volume (m³) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Thickness (m)
Example: 6 m × 4 m × 0.12 m = 2.88 m³ (garage slab, 12 cm thick)

Important: keep thickness in metres, not centimetres. A 12 cm thickness is 0.12 m — forget the conversion and you'll come out with 288 m³, which is a hint you should start over.

Example 1: garage slab 6 × 4 m

Standard garage, 6 by 4 metres, reinforced concrete slab 12 cm thick with a mesh.

CalculationResult
Length × Width × Thickness6.0 × 4.0 × 0.12
Net volume2.88 m³
Margin 10% (uneven subgrade, spillage)+0.29 m³
Order3.20 m³

Ready-mix trucks in NL deliver in increments of 0.5 m³ (sometimes 0.25 m³). So you'd order 3.25 m³ — the supplier rounds up.

Example 2: strip foundation

Strip foundation under a 5 × 4 m extension, with a trench 30 cm wide and 80 cm deep, running along the full perimeter.

Foundation formula
Volume = Perimeter × Width × Depth
Perimeter of 5×4 rectangle = 2×(5+4) = 18 m
CalculationResult
Perimeter18.0 m
Trench width × depth0.30 × 0.80
Net volume4.32 m³
Margin 8% (soil slumps into trench, rough edges)+0.35 m³
Order4.70 m³

Note: if there's a strip foundation under an internal load-bearing wall, that extra run counts too. Measure the total length of all trenches, not just the external perimeter.

Example 3: sloped driveway

Driveway 8 m long × 3 m wide, with a small height difference for drainage towards the street. Standard driveway thickness: 15 cm, but the slope means the thickness varies between the top and bottom of the run.

Formula with slope
Volume = L × W × Average thickness
Average thickness = (Thickness at top + Thickness at bottom) / 2

Say the driveway is 15 cm at the garage end and 12 cm at the street (3 cm slope over 8 metres). Average thickness = (0.15 + 0.12) / 2 = 0.135 m.

CalculationResult
L × W × average thickness8.0 × 3.0 × 0.135
Net volume3.24 m³
Margin 8%+0.26 m³
Order3.50 m³

💡 Don't forget to measure the formwork

Using formwork? The inside dimensions matter, not the outside. A box of 8×3 m with a 22 mm plank on all sides has a net area of 7.96 × 2.96 m. Small difference, but at 15 cm thickness that's already nearly 20 litres of concrete.

Why always order 5–10% extra

Every experienced concrete worker adds a margin. The reason: theory versus site reality.

  • Uneven subgrade — especially with slabs, concrete settles into dips
  • Losses during pour — some stays in the truck, some falls beside the formwork
  • Shrinkage during curing — concrete loses volume as it dries, ~1–2%
  • Measurement errors — a trench often turns out 5 cm deeper on site than on paper
  • Extra callout — a second truck run (call-out fee + minimum order) is around €200. For that money you order 1 m³ extra you'll rarely need to dispose of

Rules of thumb: slab 10%, foundation 8%, driveway 8%. With very uneven ground or rough excavation, 12–15% is wiser.

Rule of thumb: below 1 m³ you mix yourself (or get a small mini-mix delivered). Above 1.5 m³ you order ready-mix. Between 1 and 1.5 m³ it's a judgement call: do you have help, is the site accessible to a truck, and are you in a hurry?

⚠️ Ready-mix = one shot

Truck arrives, you have roughly ninety minutes before it starts to set. Make sure formwork, reinforcement, level subgrade, screed rail and helpers are ready before the truck pulls up. Otherwise you're stuck with a half-filled box and hardening concrete — not a fun morning.

Summary

Volume = Length × Width × Thickness. Add an 8–10% margin for unevenness, losses and measurement errors. Below 1 m³ mix yourself; above 1.5 m³ order ready-mix.
