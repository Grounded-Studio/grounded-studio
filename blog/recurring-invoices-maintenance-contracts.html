Recurring Invoices for Maintenance Contracts: A Practical Guide for Small Contractors

📅 27 September 2026⏱ 7 min read✍️ Grounded Studio

You have regular clients. Monthly garden maintenance for Mrs. Van der Berg. Quarterly window cleaning for the dental practice around the corner. Annual boiler check for ten families in your neighbourhood. Stable revenue — except that every single time you have to retype the invoice, or worse, you forget to send it.

In this guide: how to turn every regular client into a recurring template, which cadence fits which job, what to actually put in the description, and why a dashboard reminder makes more difference than you think.

Why a template beats duplicating

Most sole traders I speak to do it like this: they grab last month's invoice, change the date, send it. Works — until you forget. Or until you have twenty clients and every first-of-the-month you're stuck for an hour just duplicating invoices.

A recurring template is nothing more than an invoice you set up once, linked to: the client, the amount, the description, and how often you want to send it. Weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly. The app remembers the next due date and puts it on your dashboard as soon as it's time.

💡 Not sure? Start monthly

If you don't have a rhythm with a client yet, monthly is almost always the safest choice. Your client keeps overview, you see quickly whether they pay. You can always adjust the cadence later.

What actually belongs in the description

A good description prevents disputes. "Maintenance" is too vague. "Garden maintenance September 2026 — mowing, hedge trimming, leaf removal" is clear. For a recurring template, a description with a time reference that moves with it works best:

  • Put the month or quarter in the description so the client can match their own bookkeeping
  • Name the concrete tasks — especially for residential clients who want to see the detail
  • Reference the maintenance agreement if you have one (contract number or start date)
  • For variable work: keep the baseline fixed, add extra lines when it's incidental

Example for a gardener: "Monthly garden maintenance – September 2026. Mowing, edging, weeding front garden. Per contract dated March 2026." Next month you change the month, the rest stays.

Worked example: what maintenance actually brings in

Suppose you're a sole trader doing garden maintenance. You have 15 regular clients, each taking service 8 months a year (March through October).

That's 15 clients × 8 invoices = 120 invoices per year. If each invoice takes 5 minutes to type manually, that's 10 hours a year on typing alone. With recurring templates it drops to roughly 1 hour — the rest is one-tap send.

How to do this in BuildExact

  1. Create the client first under Clients. Add name, address and mobile number — you'll need that mobile number in a moment for WhatsApp sending.
  2. Create a new invoice for that client the usual way (lines, price, VAT rate).
  3. Save it as a recurring template using the "Save as recurring template" button and pick a cadence (weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly).
  4. Set the first due date — usually the first of the following month, or the end of your current service period.
  5. Wait for the dashboard reminder. When it's time, you'll see it at the top of your dashboard. Tap it, check the description, send via WhatsApp or email as PDF.

The client then receives a tidy WhatsApp message with the PDF invoice attached — your Dutch mobile number is automatically converted to the international 316 format WhatsApp expects, so you don't have to think about it.

⚠️ Adjust the next due date before you go on holiday

Going away for two weeks? Push the next invoice date manually a few weeks back. Otherwise a reminder is quietly stacking up while you're at the beach. The template follows whatever date you set.

What if a client asks for extra work mid-month

Situation you'll hit often: alongside the fixed monthly garden visit, the client also had a tree removed this month. What do you do? Two options:

  • Separate invoice for the extra work, template stays as-is. Cleaner for your bookkeeping, more paperwork for the client.
  • Add an extra line to the recurring invoice for this month. Faster, one payment. You don't change the template — next month it's back to standard.

The second option is usually more convenient for sole traders. Important: if you edit the template itself, the change sticks. If you edit only the invoice generated from the template, it applies only to that instance.

Summary

Duplicating the same invoice every month is wasted time. Set up your regular clients once as recurring templates, pick the cadence that fits the work (weekly to yearly), keep the description specific with a time reference, and let the dashboard remind you. With fifteen regular clients you'll save roughly nine hours a year — and you'll never forget one again.
